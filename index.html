Understanding EMG-Controlled Prosthetic Hands

Imagine being able to control a prosthetic hand using electrical signals from your own muscles. Researchers are working on technology that could make this possible and help people with limb loss perform everyday tasks more independently.

This website looks at how electromyography (EMG) and artificial intelligence can help prosthetic hands recognize and respond to human movements.

About the Research

My research focuses on three different parts of EMG-controlled prosthetics: recognizing muscle signals, testing how prosthetic hands perform, and using feedback to improve control.

These topics are connected because recognizing a muscle signal is only the first step. A prosthetic hand must also respond correctly and be practical for someone to use in everyday life.
